CSPC Lost & Found

About Back2You

Find what's lost, return what's found, and let it all come Back2You.

Back2You gives the CSPC community one simple place to report lost belongings, share found-item information, and help items make their way back to their owners.

A simple process

How to use Back2You

Start by checking the listings. If you cannot find what you need, add a clear report so others can help.

🔎

1. Search the listings

Browse lost and found items. Search by words and narrow results by item type, category, location, or status.

📝

2. Report an item

Choose whether you lost or found something. Add its name, category, campus location, description, and your contact details. Photos are optional and must be JPG, PNG, GIF, or WebP files no larger than 5 MB.

🤝

3. Verify and return it

If a found listing may match your lost-item report, sign in to submit ownership details. Campus administrators review claim requests.

For registered accounts

Extra ways to protect your belongings

Signing in unlocks private tools that are not part of the public lost-and-found list.

🔐

A private belongings vault

Register items you own and keep their records in your account. These belongings are not shown in the public item listings.

🏷️

A QR recovery sticker

Each registered belonging can have a QR sticker. If someone finds it and scans the code, they can send you a message through Back2You.

🔔

Account alerts

Messages from people who scan your item's QR code appear in your account notifications, so they can reach you without seeing your phone number or email address.

Student self-registration uses a CSPC student email ending in @my.cspc.edu.ph and requires a valid college department. Your password must be at least 8 characters and include an uppercase letter, a lowercase letter, a number, and a special symbol.

Good to know

Important limits and safe-use tips

  • Hand in found property. Please take found items to the Student Affairs Office or a nearby campus security guard. An online report helps the owner find the listing; it does not replace handing over the item.
  • Keep one ownership clue private. Describe an item clearly, but do not publish every identifying detail. Keep a unique mark or detail to help verify the real owner.
  • Claims need an account and a matching report. A claim request is available to the account that reported the matching lost item. An administrator reviews the request; a possible match is not proof of ownership.
  • QR codes are for messages, not tracking. They do not show an item's live location. Back2You helps people connect, but cannot guarantee that an item will be found or returned.
  • Share only what is needed. Use accurate contact details in reports, and avoid putting passwords, financial details, or other sensitive information in an item description.
